About the network

A collaborative community for researchers working on how the brain and nervous system age.

Our mission is to establish a collaborative network that brings together researchers in neuroscience, neuroengineering, and related disciplines to address the challenges of population aging in Latin America.

The network promotes interdisciplinary collaboration to better understand age-related changes in the brain and nervous system, and to develop innovative approaches that support healthy and active aging.

  • Connect researchers and institutions across the region.
  • Promote knowledge exchange and training, particularly among early-career scientists.
  • Foster interdisciplinary research collaborations on different aspects of brain and nervous system aging.
Our vision is a sustainable Latin American community that connects neuroscience and neuroengineering to advance the understanding of aging and contribute to healthier aging across the region.

Why Latin America, and why now

The region is aging quickly, while the expertise to respond is spread across many institutions and countries.

A rapid demographic shift

Latin America has a fast-growing proportion of older adults. Aging brings a growing burden of neurological and cognitive conditions that affect independence, quality of life, and health systems.

Expertise that is fragmented

Neuroscience research has grown across the region, but sustained collaboration between neuroscience, neuroengineering, computational science, and other disciplines remains limited.

An opportunity to connect

Understanding healthy and pathological aging requires accessible approaches to assessment, monitoring, prevention, and intervention. Connecting existing groups strengthens regional capacity.

The activity

A founding meeting to launch the network, define how it will work, and start collaborations.

Goals and outcomes

Formally launch the network, map the regional research landscape, form thematic working groups, and define an initial governance structure and roadmap for 2027 and beyond.

Who should attend

A multidisciplinary community from across Latin America: researchers, early-career scientists, students, healthcare professionals, and institutional or industry partners in neuroscience, engineering, medicine, AI and related fields.

Format and capacity

Hybrid: 10 participants in person and 20 joining virtually, part of an initial network expected to grow to about 20–30 researchers.

Objectives

  • Map the regional landscape of aging and neuroengineering research across participating institutions.
  • Facilitate scientific exchange and identify shared research priorities.
  • Establish collaborative thematic working groups for joint projects, data sharing, and training.
  • Define the network's initial governance, membership, and communication structure.

Mexico City was selected for its strategic position within the Mexican neuroscience and research ecosystem. As the country's largest academic and scientific hub, it concentrates universities, research centers, and hospitals with expertise in neuroscience, neuroengineering, and aging.

The city also offers strong national and international connectivity, making it accessible to participants from across Mexico and other Latin American countries, while reducing logistical barriers to attendance.

Sustainability and governance

The network is designed to continue well beyond the founding meeting.

Formation and governance

The network will be established during the activity through a coordinating group, initial thematic working groups, and agreed membership and communication mechanisms, reviewed and adapted as the network grows through 2027.

Keeping momentum

Monthly virtual meetings, a regular newsletter, and continued follow-up with working groups, plus participation in regional events such as the annual Symposium on Aging and Longevity.

Existing initiatives

Builds on the organizing group's Aging and Longevity Group and the II Symposium on Aging and Longevity (March 2026, Tecnológico de Monterrey, Mexico City), which brought together more than 50 national and international experts.
